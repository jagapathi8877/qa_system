Artificial intelligence is the simulation of human intelligence processes by computer systems. These processes include learning, reasoning, problem solving, perception, and language understanding. Modern artificial intelligence research focuses on systems that can adapt to new information and improve their performance over time without being explicitly reprogrammed.

Machine learning is a branch of artificial intelligence in which a system learns patterns from data instead of following hand-written rules. The system is trained on examples, builds a statistical model, and then uses that model to make predictions or decisions on new, unseen data. Common applications include spam filtering, speech recognition, medical diagnosis, and recommendation systems.

Supervised learning is the category of machine learning in which a model is trained on labelled data, meaning each training example comes with the correct answer attached. The algorithm adjusts itself to reduce the gap between its predictions and the true labels. Typical supervised tasks are classification, such as deciding whether an email is spam, and regression, such as predicting the price of a house.

Unsupervised learning works with unlabelled data and tries to discover hidden structure on its own. Clustering algorithms such as k-means group similar examples together, while dimensionality reduction techniques like principal component analysis compress data into fewer informative features. Unsupervised methods are widely used for exploratory data analysis and anomaly detection.

TF-IDF stands for Term Frequency Inverse Document Frequency, a numerical statistic used to measure how important a word is to a document in a collection. The term frequency part counts how often a word appears in a document, while the inverse document frequency part down-weights words that appear in almost every document. Multiplying the two gives a weight that is high for words that are frequent in one document but rare across the collection, which makes TF-IDF useful for information retrieval and text mining.

Cosine similarity measures the angle between two vectors and is commonly used to compare documents in vector space. A cosine similarity of one means the vectors point in exactly the same direction, while a value of zero means they share nothing. Because cosine similarity ignores the length of the vectors, a short question can be compared fairly against a long paragraph.

A confusion matrix is a table used to evaluate the performance of a classification model. Its rows represent the true classes and its columns represent the predicted classes, producing counts of true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives. From these counts practitioners derive metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and the F1 score.

Overfitting happens when a machine learning model learns the training data too well, including its noise and random quirks, and therefore performs poorly on new data. An overfitted model has low training error but high test error. Common remedies include collecting more training data, simplifying the model, adding regularisation, and using techniques such as cross-validation to detect the problem early.

A neural network is a computational model loosely inspired by the human brain, built from layers of interconnected units called neurons. Each connection carries a weight that is adjusted during training, and each neuron applies an activation function to the weighted sum of its inputs. Deep learning refers to neural networks with many hidden layers, which have achieved state of the art results in image recognition, speech processing, and language modelling.

Natural language processing, often abbreviated to NLP, is the field of artificial intelligence that gives computers the ability to understand and generate human language. Core NLP tasks include tokenization, part of speech tagging, named entity recognition, sentiment analysis, machine translation, and question answering. Tokenization is the step of splitting raw text into individual words or subword units, which is usually the first stage of any text processing pipeline.

Lemmatization reduces a word to its dictionary form, called a lemma, so that running, runs, and ran are all mapped to the base form run. Stopword removal discards very common function words such as the, is, and of, because they carry little meaning on their own. Together these preprocessing steps shrink the vocabulary and let similarity algorithms focus on the informative words of a document.

Question answering systems come in two broad flavours. Extractive systems locate the span of text inside a document that answers the question and return it directly, while abstractive systems generate a new sentence in their own words. Retrieval based question answering first narrows the corpus down to the most relevant passages using similarity search, and only then extracts or composes the final answer.

William Shakespeare was an English playwright and poet born in 1564 in Stratford upon Avon, widely regarded as the greatest writer in the English language. He wrote around thirty seven plays, including tragedies such as Hamlet, Macbeth, and King Lear, as well as comedies like A Midsummer Night's Dream. Hamlet, written around 1600, tells the story of a Danish prince who seeks revenge for the murder of his father, the king.

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy. Using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide, plants produce glucose and release oxygen as a by-product. Photosynthesis takes place mainly in the leaves, inside organelles called chloroplasts that contain the green pigment chlorophyll.

The solar system consists of the Sun and everything that orbits around it, including eight planets, dozens of moons, and countless asteroids and comets. The four inner planets, Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars, are small and rocky, while the outer planets, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune, are giants composed mostly of gas and ice. Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only known world to support life.

To train a supervised model, first collect and clean a labelled dataset, then split it into a training set and a test set. Next, choose a suitable algorithm and fit it to the training data while tuning its hyperparameters. Finally, evaluate the model on the test set using metrics appropriate for the task, and retrain with the chosen settings before deploying it to production.

Cross-validation is a resampling technique for assessing how well a model generalises. The dataset is divided into k equal parts, the model is trained on k minus one of them and tested on the remaining part, and the process is repeated until every part has served as the test set once. Averaging the k scores gives a more reliable estimate of real world performance than a single train test split.

Sentiment analysis is the NLP task of determining the emotional tone behind a piece of text, classifying it as positive, negative, or neutral. Companies use sentiment analysis on product reviews, social media posts, and survey responses to measure public opinion at scale. Approaches range from lexicon based methods that count positive and negative words to machine learning classifiers trained on annotated examples.
